Modern India · 2008

Chandrayaan Missions

CHANDRAYAAN-1 · 2008CHANDRAYAAN-2 · 2019CHANDRAYAAN-3 · 2023

Chandrayaan-1, launched on 22 October 2008 aboard a PSLV rocket, orbited the Moon and carried instruments from ISRO, NASA, ESA and Bulgaria.

Its Moon Impact Probe and the NASA-supplied Moon Mineralogy Mapper together provided evidence of hydroxyl and water molecules on the lunar surface, a landmark finding in planetary science.

Chandrayaan-2, launched on 22 July 2019, placed an orbiter successfully around the Moon, though its Vikram lander crashed during descent in September 2019.

Chandrayaan-3, launched on 14 July 2023 aboard the LVM3 rocket, corrected that setback: its Vikram lander touched down in the Moon's south polar highlands on 23 August 2023 at 6:04 pm IST, deploying the Pragyan rover the next day.

The achievement made India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to do so near the lunar south pole — a region prized for its permanently shadowed craters that may hold water ice.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi later named the landing site 'Shiva Shakti'.

Key discoveries

  • Chandrayaan-1 launched 22 October 2008; helped confirm water molecules on the Moon
  • Chandrayaan-2 launched 22 July 2019; its orbiter succeeded even though the Vikram lander crashed
  • Chandrayaan-3 launched 14 July 2023 on the LVM3 rocket from Sriharikota
  • Vikram lander soft-landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, at 6:04 pm IST
  • India became the fourth nation to soft-land on the Moon and the first near the south pole
  • The Vikram lander and Pragyan rover carried instruments to study lunar soil, seismic activity and the thin atmosphere
  • 23 August is now marked in India as National Space Day
