Flourishing across the vast alluvial basins of the Indus, Ghaggar-Hakra, and coastal Saurashtra, Harappan civilization pioneered modular kiln-fired brick architecture, orthogonal street planning, and civic sanitation centuries ahead of contemporary societies.

c. 2500 BCECivic Engineering
Mohenjo-daro (Sindh, Pakistan)

Construction of the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro

Civic engineers on the Western Citadel mound construct a 12-by-7 metre baked brick tank sealed with natural bitumen. Designed with descending stairways, surrounding colonnades, and dedicated drainage conduits, it represents humanity’s earliest known public ritual water structure.

Source: Excavations at Mohenjo-daro (Mackay, E.J.H. (1938), Government of India ASI Excavations)

c. 2200 BCEMaritime Commerce
Lothal (Gujarat, India)

Tidal Dockyard & Carnelian Bead Ateliers of Lothal

Harappan engineers construct a trapezoidal brick tidal basin connected to the Gulf of Khambhat via river channels. The settlement becomes a world centre for precision-drilled carnelian beads and stone seals exported across the Persian Gulf to Dilmun and Magan.

Source: Lothal: A Harappan Port Town (1955–62) (Rao, S.R. (1979), Memoirs of the Archaeological Survey of India)
